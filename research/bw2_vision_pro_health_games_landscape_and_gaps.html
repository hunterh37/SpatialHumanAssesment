Apple Vision Pro: health, exercise, coaching and games apps

What exists and where the gaps are, with a focus on medical settings and nursing homes. Research done October 4, 2026, for the SpatialAge hackathon app (reaction time, reach and spatial memory as a measure of functional aging).

Bottom line. About 200 apps and deployments were checked across seven areas: medical, fitness, wellness, games, two-player games, learning apps and senior-living VR.
  • Vision Pro health use is dominated by surgeons and radiologists on the clinical side and calm, passive meditation on the consumer side.
  • Exercise, games and brain training exist only as small independent apps with very few ratings.
  • Nothing on Vision Pro is designed for older adults. None of the senior-living VR companies (Rendever, Mynd Immersive, Viva Vita, Penumbra, XRHealth's Medicare product) runs on it, and no nursing-home pilot has been published.
  • No app measures anything a clinician could use. None reports reaction time, reach or spatial memory against age norms, keeps a score over time, or has a caregiver or clinician dashboard.
That is exactly the space SpatialAge targets. The biggest risk is the platform itself, not the competition: Apple has reportedly stopped work on future Vision Pro hardware.

1e. Two-player games

For older adults, the question is how a resident plays with a family member or staff member. There are five ways two people can play on Vision Pro:

  • Remote: FaceTime with SharePlay, the other player appearing as a life-size avatar (Persona).
  • Same room: two Vision Pros side by side.
  • Cross-play: the second person plays on an iPhone, iPad or Mac.
  • Online: matched against strangers.
  • Pass-and-play: one device handed back and forth.

"Likely" below means the app supports SharePlay, which Apple says also works for people in the same room, but the developer doesn't say so.

Two-player gaps.
  • Most games need both players to own a Vision Pro. Most families won't.
  • Game Room is the only clear "resident in the headset, grandchild on a phone" game: turn-based classics, look and pinch.
  • Only four games say they support two headsets in the same room. These are PersonaChess, PersonasFlip, Pickle Pro and Spatial Rifts, and the two physical ones lean on $250 controllers that frail hands can't easily grip.
  • No two-player game is made for older adults or measures thinking or movement.
An opening for SpatialAge: a Vision Pro side for the resident plus an iPhone or iPad side for the partner over SharePlay, played turn by turn or at a slow pace, with no controllers. It could be, say, a reach-and-recall game in which the grandchild sets the pattern on a phone and the resident reproduces it in 3D. Every round would then also be a measurement.

Learning gaps.
  • Good fits for older adults already exist. The calm, narrated, seated look-and-pinch apps suit them: D-Day, Polio's Last Mile, Museas, Museum Alive, REWILD, Astronoma, Exploring Mars and Spatial Earth.
  • Hands-on skill coaching is rare. Only Simply Piano and Drawing Desk coach a skill step by step. Languages, guitar, drums, coding and trivia exist only as flat iPad apps.
  • Missing: a language-learning app built for the headset, bridge, spatial trivia or memory games, and cooking-technique teaching.
  • Nothing is designed for older adults. No app has large text, slow pacing, or topics from their own lifetimes (the space race, the 1950s to 1970s, favorite music).
For SpatialAge, a "learn something, then recall where it was" format (for example, exhibits placed around the room in a mini museum, then asked about later) would turn a learning app into a spatial memory test.

2. The obvious gaps

Gaps that apply directly to SpatialAge
  1. No measurement app at all. Nothing on Vision Pro reports reaction time in milliseconds, reach range or spatial memory, compares results with age norms, or tracks a score over time. Existing apps report game scores, calories or meditation minutes. The closest are Brain Experiment, Focus and Reflexy, and none claims validation.
  2. Nothing designed for older adults. No visionOS app across all four categories markets to seniors, uses large text or simplified onboarding for them, or reports results by age.
  3. No caregiver or clinician view. The best today is in-app stats (Game Room) or Apple Health workout logging (Ping Pong Club, FunFitLand). Nothing shares results with family or a clinician.
  4. No 3D Corsi or object-location memory task, and no hand-tracked reach test. These are validated on tablets and on other headsets. A VR spatial memory task separated MCI from healthy older adults with 0.94 sensitivity and 0.96 specificity (PMC9408476). Meta-analyses put VR detection of MCI at about 0.88 sensitivity and specificity (PubMed 41293102).
  5. No published Vision Pro study in older adults. Every Vision Pro user study found used young volunteers or clinicians. A feasibility study would be new: comfort, how often eye tracking fails with drooping eyelids or cataracts, cybersickness, and whether scores repeat on retest.
Wider gaps (other teams' opportunities, or later add-ons)
  1. Senior-living platform on Vision Pro. No Rendever or Mynd equivalent: reminiscence, group travel, a staff tablet that runs several headsets at once.
  2. Balance, gait and fall risk. Nothing exists, and visionOS has no body or leg tracking (visionOS 27 adds tracked accessories only) (UploadVR).
  3. Rehab with measurement. No stroke, physical therapy or occupational therapy app on Vision Pro; all rehab VR vendors are on Quest or VIVE.
  4. Gentle guided exercise for seniors (seated tai chi or chair exercise with feedback). Only Kung Fu Panda comes close.
  5. Family connection for people whose family doesn't own a Vision Pro. Personas require peers with headsets. Game Room cross-play with a grandchild's iPhone is the only bridge found.
  6. Reminiscence therapy. Photos "spatial scenes" turns old family photos into 3D, but nobody packages it for memory care.
  7. Shared-headset workflow for residents. visionOS 26 lets a team share headsets, but each person's setup is saved to their own iPhone or iCloud. Residents without iPhones have no supported path (Apple 124965).
  8. Billing route. Rehab VR bills Medicare through E1905 or CPT 0770T. No Vision Pro product uses cognitive-assessment or remote-monitoring billing codes.

Enablers:

  • HealthKit: works on visionOS (WWDC24).
  • ResearchKit: a Stanford fork supports visionOS (GitHub).
  • Shared use: Guest User mode, team device sharing in visionOS 26, and remote device management that resets a headset for the next person (Apple deployment guide).
  • Accessibility: Dwell Control and Pointer Control.
  • No controllers to grip: hand tracking only.

4. What this means for SpatialAge

  • Positioning: "the first functional aging measurement on Vision Pro". The incumbents sell entertainment and therapy sessions, not a repeatable measurement.
  • Framing: keep it as wellness or research use. A diagnostic claim would likely make it FDA-regulated software. Brain Experiment and Focus both avoid clinical claims.
  • Demo hooks judges will recognize: a 3D Corsi task (validated on paper and tablets, never shipped on Vision Pro), hand-tracked reach range, and a longitudinal score sent to a clinician or family dashboard. The repo's services/ingest and apps/dashboard already point that way.
  • Answer to "why Vision Pro?": hand tracking with no controllers to grip, and passthrough so the person still sees the room. The scoring should be portable to other headsets.
  • Cheap evidence: a small test-retest and comfort study in older adults would be the first published Vision Pro study in that group.

5. Caveats

  • A listing that says "Requires visionOS" does not mean the app was built for Vision Pro, because iPhone and iPad apps show the same line. The research files mark each app as native, compatibility mode or uncertain.
  • Some App Store pages were rate limited, and a few numbers come from search summaries. Check exact figures before putting them on a slide.
  • The App Store has no public full catalog, so this is a broad survey, not a complete census. Uncertain entries: Reflexy and Spatial Solitaire (404), Calm (no native app). Mindway has been removed from the US App Store.
  • Prices are US App Store prices checked on October 4, 2026, and change often. "Free + IAP" means free to download with paid in-app purchases or a subscription; the price shown is the main one.
  • The Vision Pro hardware news comes from press reports, not Apple.
